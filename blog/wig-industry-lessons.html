What Ten Years in the Wig Industry Actually Taught Me

On method, patience, and building a technique nobody could copy.

According to Jess

I didn't set out to build a signature technique. I set out to solve a problem — women wanted beautiful, natural-looking hair without sitting in a chair for hours or dealing with glue every single day. Ten years later, that problem-solving turned into a method that's entirely my own.

The lesson nobody tells you about mastering a craft is that the first few years aren't about skill. They're about repetition nobody sees. Late nights. Redoing the same install a dozen different ways until it finally clicks. That's the part of the journey that doesn't make it into the finished photos.

Patience isn't the absence of ambition. It's what ambition looks like when it's actually working.

What I built at So GorJess didn't come from a workshop or a certification. It came from paying attention — to what worked, what didn't, and what women actually needed versus what the industry assumed they wanted. That's still how I build everything, hair or otherwise.
